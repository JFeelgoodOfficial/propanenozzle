## What it is

ELAFLEX PACIFIC introduced the GasGuard GG40 for the US and Canadian markets as a straightforward, recognized nozzle for Quick Connect (K15) propane filling. Where the GasGuard GG1 and GG20 screw onto a 1¾" ACME thread, the GG40 pushes onto the K15 receptacle and locks. ELAFLEX built it to carry the GasGuard reputation for reliability and user comfort into the K15 era.

## How K15 filling works

The operator pushes the nozzle over the vehicle's K15 fill valve until the locking segments engage, then pulls the lever. The GG40 uses larger locking segments for a secure connection. The design gives low effort at the lever, lower than ELAFLEX's own ACME nozzles, and releases less than 1 cm³ of propane when the nozzle comes off. ELAFLEX states that personal protective equipment is not necessary for the GG40 unless local codes or regulations require it.

# GG40 vs GG20: K15 Quick Connect vs 1¾" ACME, Which Nozzle Your Vehicles Need
URL: https://propanenozzle.com/guides/gg40-vs-gg20-acme-vs-k15.html
Product we sell.

Summary: Match the nozzle to the fill valve on the vehicle. Autogas vehicles built or converted after 1 January 2020 must carry a K15 quick-connect valve under NFPA 58, and those take the GG40. Older ACME vehicles, forklift cylinders and RVs take a 1¾" ACME nozzle such as the GG20. The GG40 flows up to 17 GPM and releases under 1 cm³ on disconnect; the GG20 flows 63 L/min (about 16.6 GPM) and releases 1.9 cm³. Adapters between the two are discouraged. Propane Nozzle sells the GG40; the GG20 guides stay online as reference.

## The rule that drives the change

NFPA 58, from its 2017 edition, requires the K15 quick-connect refueling valve on autogas vehicles produced or converted after 1 January 2020. The rule is not retroactive: an existing ACME-equipped vehicle keeps its valve until it is converted, bought or brought back into service. Industry groups report the push-on K15 connection releases far less propane per disconnect than the threaded ACME connection; Alliance AutoGas puts the reduction at about 76%, a trade figure rather than an independent test.

## Mixed fleets

A station serving both vehicle generations needs both nozzle types, which usually means a dual-hose dispenser with one K15 hose and one ACME hose, or two dispensers. Adapters that let a K15 nozzle fill an ACME valve exist, but NFPA guidance and the trade press treat them as a stopgap, and ELAFLEX advises against fill-point adapters on its ACME nozzles because they raise the gas released on disconnect. Plan the hanging hardware around the valves you actually fill.

## How to tell which valve a vehicle has

An ACME valve is a threaded brass fitting about 1¾" across, usually with a plastic dust cap that screws on. A K15 valve is a smooth-bodied receptacle with no external thread, and its cap pushes on. Vehicle build or conversion paperwork dated 2020 or later almost always means K15.

## Connecting the nozzle to the hose

- Match the hose-end thread to the nozzle inlet (¾" NPT on distributor-listed GG40s).

- No PTFE tape. ELAFLEX's GasGuard manual gives two reasons: poor electrical conductivity across the joint, and tape particles breaking loose into the flow path. Use a non-permanent liquid thread sealant.

- Tighten with spanners on the flats, never a pipe wrench on the nozzle body.

- Pressurize and check every joint with a foaming leak-detection agent.

- Run an operational fill on a K15 receptacle and confirm no leaks at the nozzle, the pull-away and the swivel.

## Pull-away placement

Mount the ARK 19.3 between the whip and the delivery hose, with its lanyard anchored to the dispenser or a fixing point that can take more than the coupling's maximum separation force. After a drive-off, check both halves and the hose before reconnecting.

## Reed-switch dispensers

Some dispensers start the pump from a reed switch in the nozzle boot that senses a magnet in the nozzle. ELAFLEX has not yet published whether the GG40 offers a magnet option; tell us when you order if your boot works that way and we'll confirm.

## NFPA 58 and the K15 valve

The K15 requirement entered NFPA 58 in the 2017 edition and applies to vehicles produced or converted after 1 January 2020; older vehicles are not retroactively affected. The 2024 edition, per Butane-Propane News, lets Euro-style push-on nozzles be used without the operator training the ACME connection required. State adoption of NFPA 58 editions varies, so check which edition your state enforces.

## UL approval and the latch

ELAFLEX states that both the latched and unlatched GG40 are UL approved. That removes the latch question that follows the ACME GasGuards, where the hold-open latch sits outside the UL 125 listing. A site's authority having jurisdiction can still restrict latched nozzles at self-service sites, so ask before specifying latched nozzles for the public.

## PPE

ELAFLEX's brochure states personal protective equipment is not necessary for the GG40 unless required by local codes or regulations. Many operators keep gloves and eye protection as site policy regardless; follow your site rules.

## Emissions figures

- ELAFLEX: the GG40 releases less than 1 cm³ of propane on disconnect, which it calls ultra-low emission.

- Trade press (Alliance AutoGas): the average K15 connection releases about 76% less fugitive emissions than ACME. Treat this as an industry claim, not an independent measurement.

- For comparison, ELAFLEX rates its ACME GG20 at 1.9 cm³ per disconnect.

## LPG hazards to train for

Propane is heavier than air and pools low; liquid propane causes cold burns on skin. If a leak or uncontrolled release occurs during filling: release the lever, hit the dispenser emergency stop, move people away, and call emergency services. Train everyone who fills on the site's emergency procedure before they fill.

# How to Fill Forklift Cylinders and RVs with a GasGuard GG20
URL: https://propanenozzle.com/guides/how-to-fill-forklift-cylinders-and-rvs-with-gg20.html
Reference only (not sold).

Summary: To use a GG20: shut off the engine, check the fill point and nozzle seals are clean and undamaged, put on gloves and safety glasses, align the nozzle with the fill valve, screw the connector nut clockwise until firm, pull the lever to fill, and release it to stop. When finished, release the lever, unscrew the nut counter-clockwise keeping hands clear of the small gas release, and return the nozzle to its boot. Stop immediately if gas escapes continuously. The nozzle does not control fill level; follow the filling procedure for the container.

## Before you start

- No open flames, smoking, static sources, mobile phones or other electrical devices in the transfer area.

- Engine off.

- Gloves and safety glasses on. LPG is extremely cold when it depressurizes and can cause cold burns.

- Look at the fill point and the nozzle: clean, no dents, no sharp edges, seals in place, lever moves freely.

## Filling: the manufacturer's sequence

- Take the nozzle from the dispenser boot.

- Line the nozzle up with the fill point. On a recessed forklift or RV valve, keep it square; the long guided connector nut is there to help.

- Screw the connector nut clockwise until firm.

- If your nozzle has a latch, engage it with your forefinger.

- Pull the lever to start. If you see or hear leakage, let go of the lever.

- Release the lever when filling is complete. Unlatch first if latched.

- Unscrew the connector nut counter-clockwise. Keep hands away from the coupling while the trapped liquid between nozzle and fill valve vents. A small release here is normal.

- Return the nozzle to its boot.

## Forklift motor fuel cylinders

Cylinder cradles and brackets often sit around the filler valve, which is why a standard 125 mm nozzle can bottom out before the threads engage. The GG20's extra 35 mm reaches past the cradle. The nozzle only moves fuel; it does not stop at a fill level. Fill the cylinder by the method your site procedure and the cylinder require, such as by weight or by the fixed maximum liquid level gauge, and only if the cylinder is within its requalification date.

## RVs and motorhomes

RV fill valves are often behind an access door or skirting. Same procedure. Note that South Coast AQMD Rule 1177, which mandates low-emission connectors in its district, exempts cylinders dedicated to and installed on recreational vehicles.

## Stop and isolate if

- Gas escapes continuously or uncontrollably: release the lever, press the dispenser emergency stop, clear the area.

- The connection doesn't go on smoothly: disconnect and reconnect. Never force it.

- A problem repeats: take the nozzle out of service and have trained personnel inspect it. See troubleshooting.

## Q: Is a small hiss of gas normal when disconnecting a GG20?
A: Yes. ELAFLEX states a small release on uncoupling is normal: 1.9 cm³ for the GG20 and 1.7 cm³ for the GG20H and GG20DN. A continuous release is not normal.

## Q: Does the GG20 stop automatically when the tank is full?
A: No. It is a manual lever nozzle. Fill level is controlled by the filling procedure for the container, not by the nozzle.

## When to reject a part

- Distortion or damage on any sealing surface.

- Wear marks, scoring or plating damage on swivel bodies.

- Damage to the nose piece tail ramp or bridge.

- Worn lever slipper faces; worn latch pivot pin or contact points.

- Damaged strainer mesh or ring seal.

The manual says age, wear and abuse can make repair uneconomic, and replacing whole assemblies or the nozzle may be the better choice.

## Nose pieces on the GG20, GG20H and GG20DN

The nose piece is the only part that differs between the three versions, so it's the part to get right on a repair. The repair manual names the GG6 nose piece as part of the GG27 connector assembly; that is the single nose piece used on the standard GG20. The manual we cite doesn't list part numbers for the hybrid (GG20H) or dual (GG20DN) nose pieces. ELAFLEX PACIFIC publishes a separate "Replacement Nose Piece" document and a "Nose Piece O-ring information sheet" on its GasGuard information page.

Fitting the nose piece needs the special nose piece assembly tool listed above, and the white nose piece O-ring is one of the parts the 24-month seal kit replaces.

## Static test (before fitting connector and lever)

- Connect to a supply hose with an upstream isolating valve.

- Pressurize to 1200 to 1800 kPa (12 to 18 bar, 174 to 260 psi).

- Immerse in detergent-loaded water with the nozzle closed.

- Apply about 27 N·m (20 ft·lb) bending moment to the inlet swivel while rotating it slowly; watch every joint.

- Fit connector and lever; couple to an approved vehicle connector or blanked adaptor. Confirm it locks firmly and the lever and latch work.

## Dynamic flow test (with propane)

- Connect to a vehicle connector on a dispenser vapor return line with a downstream receiving tank.

- With the valve closed, check for vapor leaks with detergent spray.

- Pull the lever; spray the connector sides again.

- Release the lever; confirm only a small amount of gas escapes.

- Close the isolating valve, operate the lever to depressurize, remove the nozzle and refit the connector saddle.

If LPG isn't available, the manual allows bottled nitrogen at an 1800 kPa regulator setting, or compressed air.

# GG20 Troubleshooting: Leaks, Hard Connections, No Flow, Stiff Lever
URL: https://propanenozzle.com/guides/gg20-troubleshooting.html
Reference only (not sold).

Summary: A small gas release on disconnect is normal (1.7 to 1.9 cm³). A continuous leak is not: release the lever, hit the emergency stop, clear the area and take the nozzle out of service. If the nozzle won't thread on smoothly, disconnect and retry; never force it, and check for a damaged or worn fill point. Low or no flow usually means the coupling isn't fully engaged, a clogged strainer, or a dispenser issue. A pump that won't start on a reed-switch dispenser points to a missing J magnet.

| | Symptom | Likely cause | What to do

 | Short hiss when unscrewing | Normal release of trapped liquid between nozzle and fill valve | Nothing. Keep hands clear of the coupling.

 | Continuous or uncontrolled gas release | Damaged seal, worn nose piece, damaged fill valve | Release the lever, press the emergency stop, clear the area. Trained personnel inspect the nozzle and the receptacle. Isolate the nozzle if it recurs.

 | Nut won't thread on smoothly | Misalignment, cross-threading, dirty or damaged ACME thread on the fill point | Disconnect and reconnect square to the valve. Never force it. Inspect both threads.

 | Leak at the inlet swivel | Swivel seals worn, grub screw loose | Out of service. Swivel seals are part of the 6 to 12 month inspection and the 24-month seal kit.

 | No flow with lever pulled | Nozzle not fully coupled (the valve won't open uncoupled), pump not running | Check the nut is fully tightened and the dispenser is running.

 | Flow lower than usual | Clogged strainer, pump or hose restriction, adapter in line | Have the strainer inspected and cleaned or replaced (EK172.1). Remove adaptors if fitted.

 | Pump doesn't start when nozzle lifted | Reed-switch boot but nozzle has no magnet | Fit the J magnet option (GGJ assembly) or change the dispenser setting.

 | Lever hard to hold | Normal for single nose piece; or worn lever/valve parts | Consider the GG20H or GG20DN, which have lower lever force. If force has increased over time, inspect the valve.

 | Connector nut won't turn freely | Dirt, worn pawl or spring | Clean; have the pawl and spring checked at inspection.

Rows on "flow lower than usual" and "lever hard to hold" combine ELAFLEX's component descriptions with common service practice; they are not listed word for word in the manual. Everything involving a leak follows the manual's stop-and-isolate instruction.

# GG20 Safety, Codes and Compliance: UL 125, NFPA 58, Latches and Rule 1177
URL: https://propanenozzle.com/guides/gg20-safety-codes-and-compliance.html
Reference only (not sold).

Summary: The GG20, GG20H and GG20DN are UL listed to UL 125, and ELAFLEX states the GG1/GG20 series meet NFPA 58 among other standards. The optional hold-open latch is not covered by the UL listing and is not allowed everywhere. Release on disconnect is 1.7 to 1.9 cm³, under the 4 cm³ limit that defines a low-emission connector in South Coast AQMD Rule 1177. ## Listings and standards

- UL 125: GG20, GG20H and GG20DN are listed by Underwriters Laboratories.

- ELAFLEX's manual states GG1 and GG20 nozzles meet AS/NZS 1596, NFPA 58, AS/NZS 1425 and EN 12806.

- Check for the UL mark on the nozzle you receive, and keep the documentation.

## The latch question

The hold-open latch (L) lets the operator take their hand off the lever. ELAFLEX's data sheet states the latch is not UL listed, and its part-number guide notes that not every country or LPG authority allows one. If a quote lists a latch, decide deliberately, and ask your authority having jurisdiction first.

## Low-emission rules

South Coast AQMD Rule 1177 (Southern California) has required, since after December 31, 2013, that covered LPG transfers use low-emission connectors, defined as releasing no more than 4 cm³ of LPG on disconnect. The GG20 releases 1.9 cm³ and the GG20H and GG20DN 1.7 cm³. The rule exempts containers under 4 gallons water capacity and cylinders dedicated to recreational vehicles, among others, and requires facilities to keep documentation proving their connectors comply. Other districts may have their own rules.

## ACME versus K15 on new vehicles

Butane-Propane News reports that NFPA 58 adopted the K15 "Euro" push-to-connect filler as the style vehicle manufacturers must provide on new LPG vehicles from 2020, and that the 2024 edition lets Euro nozzles be used without specific operator training. Forklift cylinders, RVs and many existing vehicles still use 1¾" ACME fill valves, which is the GG20's job. For K15 vehicles, ELAFLEX makes the GG40, which is what Propane Nozzle now sells.

## LPG hazards to train for

- Stored as a liquid under pressure and heavier than air: it collects in low spots.

- Extremely flammable between about 2% and 10% in air.

- Extremely cold when it depressurizes: cold burns to skin and eyes. Wear gloves and safety glasses.

- Keep ignition sources out of the transfer area: flames, smoking, static, phones and electrical devices.

If there is an uncontrolled release, stop the transfer, use the emergency stop, move people away and call emergency services if needed.

A: No. ELAFLEX states the optional hold-open latch is not covered by the UL listing.

## Two hoses, two nozzles

Each hose needs its own GG20, and they don't have to match. If one lane serves the public and the other serves your own fleet, put a GG20DN on the public side and a GG20 or GG20H on the fleet side. Order the inlet size for each hose's end thread.

# GasGuard Family Compared: GG1, GG20, GG30, GG40 and ZVG 2

## GG1 or GG20?

Same function, same nose pieces, same ratings. The GG20 adds 35 mm to the connector nut. If a GG1 bottoms out on a bracket, cradle or body panel before the threads engage, you need the GG20. If it doesn't, the GG1 is lighter and shorter to handle.

## Where the others fit

The GG30 serves bayonet fill points and has a 4-slot front and redesigned nose piece; ELAFLEX states it reduces released gas on closure by 23% versus its predecessor. The GG40 is ELAFLEX's nozzle for the US and Canadian K15 quick-connect systems: up to 17 GPM, under 1 cm³ released on disconnect, lighter lever than the ACME nozzles, UL approved latched or unlatched, and it fits the same NB-GG boot. It is the nozzle Propane Nozzle sells; see the GG40 overview. The ZVG 2 EURO UL is ELAFLEX's push-on nozzle; latched versions are covered by UL 125 and UL 567.

## Q: What is the difference between GasGuard GG1 and GG20?
A: Connector reach: 125 mm on the GG1 versus 160 mm on the GG20. Everything else, including flow and nose piece options, is the same.

# Replacing a GasGuard LG20 with a GG20
URL: https://propanenozzle.com/guides/lg20-to-gg20-replacement.html
Reference only (not sold).

Summary: ELAFLEX states the GG20, GG20H and GG20DN supersede the GasGuard LG20 range. To order the right replacement, match four things from the old nozzle: the nose piece (single, hybrid or dual), the inlet thread (½" or ¾" NPT female), whether it had a latch, and whether your dispenser needs a magnet for a reed switch. Current GG20s have the strainer fitted as standard.

## What changed

ELAFLEX describes the GG20 design as having improved operational performance and reduced maintenance compared with what it replaced, with a guided connector nut on an extended thread to help alignment and a strainer fitted as standard. The repair manual for the GG20 still uses several LG-series assembly numbers, such as the LG24 valve and LG2 inlet swivel, which suggests some service parts carried over. Confirm specific spare-part compatibility before relying on it.

## Replacement checklist

 | | Check on the old nozzle | Choose on the GG20

 | Who uses it: trained operators or the public? | GG20 or GG20H for trained operators; GG20DN for public/self-service

 | Hose end thread | .2 for ½" NPT female, .3 for ¾" NPT female

 | Hold-open latch fitted? | L option, not UL listed; check your AHJ

 | Dispenser starts on lifting the nozzle via reed switch? | J magnet option

 | Boot | NB-GG holds GasGuard nozzles

# LPG Nozzle Glossary: Terms Used in GG20 Documentation
URL: https://propanenozzle.com/guides/gg20-glossary.html
Reference only (not sold).

Summary: Short definitions of the terms used across GasGuard GG20 documentation, from 1¾" ACME and nozzle reach to release volume, nose piece types, reed switches, safety breaks and UL 125.

1¾" ACME
The threaded LPG fill connection common on North American forklift cylinders, RVs and older autogas vehicles. The GG20's connector nut screws onto it.

Nozzle reach
Length of the connector nut. 160 mm on the GG20, 125 mm on the GG1.

Nose piece
The sealing front of the valve. Single ('E'), hybrid ('H') or dual ('DN') on GasGuard ACME nozzles.

Dual nose piece
ELAFLEX's patented design that seals to the fill point even if the nozzle isn't fully tightened. Used on the GG20DN.

Release volume
LPG vented when the nozzle is disconnected: the liquid trapped between nozzle valve and fill valve. 1.7 to 1.9 cm³ on the GG20 range.

Low-emission connector
Under South Coast AQMD Rule 1177, a connector releasing no more than 4 cm³ on disconnect.

Inlet swivel
The rotating joint where the hose attaches. ½" or ¾" NPT female on the GG20.

NPT
National Pipe Taper, the US tapered pipe thread standard.

Strainer
300 µm / 50 mesh screen inside the nozzle that stops debris from upstream.

Hold-open latch
A catch that holds the lever open. Optional (L) and not UL listed on the GG20.

Reed switch / magnet
Some dispensers start the pump when a magnet in the nozzle guard leaves a reed switch in the boot. Order option J for these.

Nozzle boot (NB-GG)
The enclosed holster for GasGuard nozzles on the dispenser.

Safety break / pull-away (ARK 19)
A coupling in the hose that separates and seals if a vehicle drives off while connected.

UL 125
The Underwriters Laboratories standard covering flow control valves for anhydrous ammonia and LP-gas, under which the GG20 is listed.

NFPA 58
The Liquefied Petroleum Gas Code published by the National Fire Protection Association, adopted by most US states.

AHJ
Authority having jurisdiction: the agency or official who approves an installation.

K15 / Euro connector
Push-to-connect LPG filler adopted under NFPA 58 for new vehicles. Served by the GG40 or ZVG 2, not the GG20.
